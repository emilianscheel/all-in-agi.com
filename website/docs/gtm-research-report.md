# WERKSPRUNG GTM Research Report

**How to sell Agentic Engineering Hackathons to established German companies**  
Research snapshot: 24 July 2026

## Executive recommendation

WERKSPRUNG should run a founder-led, account-based sales motion aimed at **engineering and digital leaders in German industrial and software companies**, starting with 30 named accounts and 3–4 stakeholders per account.

The most effective wedge is not “AI training” and not “a fun team event.” It is:

> **A fixed-price, one-day activation pilot that turns approved AI coding tools and real company challenges into working prototypes, internal builders, and a concrete follow-up backlog.**

This positioning matches the product already described on the website:

- one day, on site;
- 15–50 participants;
- engineering, product, and business teams together;
- current coding agents under the customer’s IT and security rules;
- real company challenges, not exercises;
- working prototypes and Demo Day;
- transparent €4,000–€6,000 net pricing, including two facilitators, challenge design, lunch, and follow-up.

The first sales objective should be **three paid lighthouse events in 90 days**, ideally one each in industrial automation, enterprise software, and digital commerce. Do not broaden to generic “AI for everyone” before WERKSPRUNG has evidence.

## 1. Why this can sell now

The DACH market has an execution gap:

- In Celonis’ 2026 survey, 85% of organizations wanted to become an “agentic enterprise” in the next two to three years, but only 17% of DACH respondents said they already used AI agents. Missing expertise was the most frequently named obstacle. This is the exact gap between ambition and hands-on ability that WERKSPRUNG can address. [Celonis, March 2026](https://www.celonis.com/de/news/press/the-enterprise-ai-reality-check-high-ambitions-meet-operational-barriers)
- Companies are already investing. Otto Group says it plans €350 million of medium-term investment in IT, tech, and AI. [Otto Group, May 2026](https://www.ottogroup.com/en/stories/story/agentic-commerce-ready-for-takeoff.php)
- AI has moved into software delivery and operations. DATEV is researching automation across the ML lifecycle and evaluating real use cases; TeamViewer is explicitly building an agentic proposition for IT support. [DATEV](https://www.datev.de/web/de/berufsgruppenuebergreifend/presse/presseinformationen/meldungen-2025/mit-ki-qualitaet-und-effizienz-in-der-softwareentwicklung-steigern), [TeamViewer](https://www.teamviewer.com/en-us/global/company/press/2025/teamviewer-reports-rapid-customer-adoption-ai/)
- Governance is now a buying requirement, not a reason to wait. Otto Group has a Responsible AI Guide; TeamViewer’s CPTO describes data governance and change management as the main gates to deploying agents. [Otto Group](https://www.ottogroup.com/de/stories/story/responsible-ai-guide.php), [TeamViewer](https://www.teamviewer.com/en-in/insights/2026-predictions-mei-dent/)

The opportunity is therefore not to persuade buyers that AI matters. It is to give a leader a safe, small, visible way to turn existing interest into action.

| Buyer | VP/Head of Engineering, CTO/CDO office, Head of Digital Products, Engineering Enablement, Innovation |
| Budget | Departmental learning, engineering enablement, innovation, or team-development budget |
| Purchase logic | Can approve €4–6k without enterprise-wide procurement |

### Negative ICP

Deprioritize:

- companies with fewer than roughly 15 relevant builders;
- organizations without an approved tool or realistic sandbox;
- buyers asking only for a keynote or general AI literacy;
- teams expecting production deployment in one day;
- consultancies that already run the same format internally, unless approached as a channel partner;
- highly regulated teams that cannot use synthetic or de-identified data and have no sandbox.

## 3. Buying committee and the job each person needs done

| Stakeholder | What they want | Likely objection | WERKSPRUNG answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| CTO / CDO | Visible progress on AI adoption | “We already have an AI strategy.” | The event operationalizes it with builders, prototypes, and a follow-up backlog. |
| VP / Head of Engineering | Faster learning and delivery | “My engineers can learn this themselves.” | The value is coordinated adoption across engineering, product, and domain experts using company challenges. |
| Engineering Enablement / Developer Experience | Repeatable tool practices | “We already rolled out Copilot.” | A license is not adoption. Measure tool use, completed prototypes, and next-step owners. |
| Head of Innovation / Digital | More validated use cases | “Hackathons create toys.” | Preselect challenges with business owners and require a user flow, live demo, feasibility note, and next step. |
| Product leader | Better discovery-to-prototype speed | “This sounds engineering-only.” | Mixed teams turn real workflows into testable product prototypes. No coding prerequisite for every participant. |
| IT Security / Data Protection | Controlled tools and data | “External AI tools are not approved.” | Use only the agreed environment, accounts, repositories, and data classes; synthetic data by default. |
| HR / L&D | Applied capability building | “Is this training?” | It produces skills through doing, but the sponsor and budget should remain in Tech/Digital where possible. |
| Procurement | Clear scope and low supplier risk | “What exactly is included?” | Fixed capacity tiers, two facilitators, challenge design, Demo Day, follow-up, and explicit exclusions. |

**Best entry point:** a director or VP close enough to the work to own 15–30 participants and senior enough to spend €5,000. The C-level executive is often a useful sponsor, not the first cold-contact target.

## 4. Target account scoring

Score each account out of 25:

- **AI signal (0–5):** public investment, product launch, job posting, or leadership statement;
- **builder density (0–5):** meaningful engineering/product/data workforce;
- **activation gap (0–5):** likely benefit from cross-functional hands-on adoption;
- **buyability (0–5):** a department can plausibly buy a €4–6k pilot;
- **proof value (0–5):** logo and case study would unlock similar customers.

Accounts scoring 20+ enter personalized outreach now. Scores are directional and should be refreshed during contact research.

## 7. The offer that should be sold

Keep the website’s three capacity prices. Package the sale around a common decision:

### “Activation Pilot”

**Before**

- 45-minute sponsor call;
- 60-minute challenge-design session with 3–5 challenge owners;
- tool, access, repository, and data-class checklist;
- participant grouping and pre-event setup test.

**During**

- short live demonstration;
- at least 70% build time;
- mixed engineering/product/domain teams;
- facilitator checkpoints;
- Demo Day scored on user value, functionality, learning, and feasible next step.

**After**

- prototype inventory;
- one-page outcome memo with participation, prototypes, tool friction, and next-step owners;
- 30-minute follow-up within ten working days.

**Commercial promise**

> “In one day, 15–50 people turn 3–8 real internal challenges into working prototypes and an evidence-based follow-up backlog—in the tool and data environment approved by your company.”

Do not promise production systems, ROI from one day, or autonomous agents deployed into live processes.

Spend no more than 25 minutes:

- map 3–4 contacts;

### 21-day sequence

| Day | Touch | Purpose |
|---:|---|---|

## 10. Discovery and qualification

The first call should be **20–30 minutes**, even though the current website offers a 60-minute intro. A first cold meeting needs lower friction; use the longer prep call only after qualification.

Ask:

1. Which AI coding or agent tools are approved, piloted, or under review?
2. Where is adoption uneven: engineering, product, or domain teams?
3. Which 3–5 workflows could be prototyped with synthetic or low-risk data?
4. Who would own a prototype after Demo Day?
5. What security or works-council constraints apply?
6. Which budget owns this: engineering enablement, innovation, L&D, or team development?
7. Is there a target date, leadership off-site, rollout, or innovation week?

Qualify out if there is no participant group, no tool path, no challenge owner, or no sponsor willing to attend Demo Day.

## 11. Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| “We already use GitHub Copilot.” | “Good—the event is not a tool introduction. It turns individual usage into cross-functional practice on company challenges and shows where adoption or governance still blocks delivery.” |
| “We can run a hackathon ourselves.” | “You should own the business context. WERKSPRUNG supplies challenge design, facilitation, frontier-tool practice, setup discipline, and the deadline pressure that gets teams to a live demo.” |
| “Security will never approve it.” | “We use only approved accounts, repositories, tools, and data classes. If no tool or sandbox can be approved, we should not schedule the event.” |
| “Hackathons produce toys.” | “Each challenge needs a business owner, user, constrained scope, live demo, and named next step. The goal is evidence and capability—not pretending a one-day prototype is production.” |
| “Our non-engineers cannot code.” | “They do not need to. Product and domain experts own the problem, user flow, and validation; engineering owns the technical path.” |
| “€5,000 is expensive for one day.” | “For 30 people that is about €167 per participant, including preparation, two facilitators, challenge design, lunch, Demo Day, and follow-up—while producing demonstrable outputs.” |
| “We need procurement first.” | “We can provide a fixed scope, data-processing position, insurance/company details, and supplier documents immediately. First let us confirm team, tool, and date.” |

## 12. Sales assets required before scaling outreach

Build these in order:

1. **Two-page PDF:** outcome, agenda, inclusions, pricing, security model, CTA.
2. **Security/setup sheet:** tool ownership, accounts, data classes, source-code rules, recording/photo rules, deletion, and responsibilities.
3. **Challenge canvas:** problem, user, current workflow, available data, risk class, prototype boundary, success test, owner.
4. **Outcome memo template:** participants, challenges, completed demos, tool friction, quotes, follow-up owners.
5. **One 90-second event video** with real builders and demos.
6. **First case study:** metrics, screenshots, sponsor quote, participant quote, what continued after 30 days.

The website currently has strong product clarity and transparent pricing but little proof. Until customer evidence exists, the founder’s credibility, careful preparation, and concrete challenge hypotheses must carry the sale.

## 13. 90-day execution plan

### Days 1–10: become sellable

- finish the six core sales assets;
- create a lightweight CRM;
- fully research the top 15 accounts and 60 contacts;
- prepare one three-challenge mini-proposal per account;
- ask personal, investor, accelerator, university, and former-colleague networks for introductions;
- reserve three event dates six to ten weeks out.

### Days 11–30: run founder-led outbound

- 5 new accounts per working day until all 30 first-wave accounts are active;
- 15–20 thoughtful first touches per week;
- 10 warm-intro requests per week;
- one relevant LinkedIn post per week showing a real prototype pattern, security practice, or challenge design lesson;
- aim for 8–10 discovery calls and 4–6 written event plans.

### Days 31–60: close and deliver lighthouse events

- close 1–2 paid events;
- include a pre-agreed evidence clause: anonymous metrics by default, named quote/photo only with approval;
- personally capture before/after confidence, prototypes completed, tool blockers, and follow-up owners;
- ask for two internal introductions immediately after Demo Day.

### Days 61–90: turn delivery into distribution

- publish the first case study within ten working days;
- send it to every open opportunity with a tailored note;
- offer a second event to a different department at the same public price;
- approach two channel partners with the proof;
- expand the account pool to 75 only after message and conversion data are clear.

## 16. Partnerships that can accelerate trust

After two strong events, pursue:

- regional industry clusters and chambers for member-company introductions;
- coding-agent vendors seeking enterprise activation after license rollout;
- cloud consultancies that sell platforms but do not run enablement events;
- corporate innovation hubs and applied research organizations;
- IT service providers that can take successful prototypes into production.

The partner proposition should be:

> “You own the platform or production implementation. WERKSPRUNG creates the one-day activation event that generates trained internal champions and qualified prototype opportunities.”

Avoid giving a partner exclusivity before it has produced paid demand.

## 17. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Buyers hear “hackathon” as entertainment | Lead with activation pilot, business owners, prototype criteria, and follow-up backlog |
| No social proof | Start with three lighthouse accounts, measure aggressively, secure evidence rights before delivery |
| Tool/security delays | Send setup sheet in the first qualified conversation; keep a synthetic-data fallback |
| Too much custom preparation for €4–6k | Standardize challenge canvas, setup checklist, agenda, and outcome memo |
| Founder travel consumes margin | Cluster outreach and event dates geographically; charge only within the website’s stated scope, then clarify travel policy |
| Internal innovation teams see overlap | Position WERKSPRUNG as execution capacity and frontier-tool facilitation; offer co-branded delivery |
| One-off revenue | Ask for the next department and a referral at Demo Day; later sell repetition without inventing confusing package tiers |
